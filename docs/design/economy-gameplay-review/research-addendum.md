# Research and economic stress example

10 November 2026. Added after the initial brief was sent to Claude; not part of that review's input.

## Moonlighter II verified source

The publisher site was blocked, but the official Steam product description was accessible:
https://store.steampowered.com/app/2350790/Moonlighter_2_The_Endless_Vault/

Local captured page text: `moonlighter-source.txt`.

It explicitly describes weapon classes, armor/perks, arranged-loot synergies, escalating risk with depth, shops with set prices/customer reading, display optimization, special events and town reinvestment. These are current published descriptions, not hands-on validation or patch-specific balance findings. Search AI summaries were not used as factual evidence.

Useful lesson: the return to town should be playable. The player should make a decision about selling, retaining, consuming or turning a find into another good, with a visible buyer or use. We should not immediately copy its backpack synergies: changing the cash value of fixed funded objects through arrangement would introduce a new economic transformation system. Begin with our existing inventory's competing uses.

A persistent town improvement or maker/shop identity can supply aspiration beyond cash. Functional identity and cosmetic collections are preferable first to permanent combat-stat inflation. Cosmetic objects still require a defined source, ownership and sink; calling them cosmetics does not remove their economic accounting.

## Maker break-even stress example

Illustrative assumptions, NOT current game tuning or forecast:

- Four fully funded copies, acquisition cost 12 coins per copy: 48 total.
- Publication/placement costs 8 total.
- Entry fee 2, no other costs or refunds; fee becomes maker income on normal termination.
- Every successful visitor extracts a full copy; unsuccessful visitors consume no pool items; no refreshes.

The maker needs 28 settled admissions to recover 56 coins. Four full extractions exhaust the inventory. Therefore, break-even requires at least 24 other settled attempts before that exhaustion. Those other attempts need not all be deaths, but this scenario makes non-extraction financially useful to the maker. Consumable use or early closure can worsen the result. Fee increases, lower acquisition costs, smaller rewards, no-treasure entertainment, or different visitation outcomes can change the economics.

This is the incentive problem a fun design must address. An attendance-only revenue rule does not by itself eliminate a preference for visitors failing to extract. Replays, trustworthy difficulty disclosures and repeat visitation can counteract it but cannot mathematically erase it. Do not promise every role profits simultaneously. In a fixed-coin economy, gains come from other participants or disclosed subsidies, and goods produced can have utility without a guaranteed cash buyer.

## Proposed acceptance before expanding scope

First show that a new player chooses another attempt for the game itself, including an explicitly no-reward practice comparison. Then observe whether real preparation choices and player demand survive ordinary equipment losses. Only then broaden catalogs, enemy families, procedural generation and maker finance tools. Testing one shove encounter is an inexpensive design experiment, not evidence the whole game is now good.

## Research method learning

Official publisher pages may be Cloudflare-blocked even when the official Steam product description is accessible in Aside. Use the latter for sourced mechanics and preserve the blocked-source note. Do not substitute search AI summaries or invent current patch details.
